# Global Linguistic & Cultural Safety Audit
*Cross-Language Linguistic Traps, False Cognates, and Pronunciation Traps*

Billion-dollar brands operate globally from Day 1. A name that sounds sleek in English can be catastrophic or comical in another language.

---

## 1. Legendary Cross-Cultural Disasters

- **Chevy Nova:** In Spanish, *"no va"* translates literally to *"it doesn't go"*. While urban legend exaggerated the sales drop, the marketing friction was real.
- **Ford Pinto:** In Brazilian Portuguese slang, *"pinto"* means tiny male genitalia. Ford had to frantically rebrand the car as the *Corcel*.
- **KFC in China:** Early translation of *"Finger-lickin' good"* was translated into Mandarin as *"Eat your fingers off"*.
- **Mitsubishi Pajero:** In Spanish-speaking Latin America, *"pajero"* is vulgar slang for a masturbator. Mitsubishi had to rename it the *Montero*.
- **Puffs Tissues:** In German, *"Puff"* is colloquial slang for a brothel / whorehouse.

---

## 2. Linguistic Checklist by Language Family

### A. Romance Languages (Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese)
- **Spanish:**
  - Watch for *mal*, *no va*, *culo*, *loco*, *pedo*, *caca*, *pito*.
  - Pronunciation: Words ending in hard consonants without vowels often get an epenthetic /e/ prepended (*E-stripe*, *E-spotify*).
- **French:**
  - Watch for *cul* (ass), *merde*, *con* (idiot), *pet* (fart).
  - Silent final consonants: Words ending in *t*, *d*, *s*, *x* are often dropped in French pronunciation.
- **Portuguese:**
  - Watch for *pinto*, *foda*, *bosta*.

### B. Germanic Languages (German, Dutch, Scandinavian)
- **German:**
  - "Gift" means *poison* in German.
  - "Mist" means *manure / crap*.
  - "Puff" means *brothel*.
  - Hard /w/ is pronounced like English /v/.
- **Dutch:**
  - "Koot" / "Kut" is severe vulgar slang.

### C. East Asian Languages (Mandarin, Japanese)
- **Mandarin Chinese:**
  - Tonal sensitivity: Every syllable (*ma, shi, fa, si*) has multiple tone meanings.
  - Number 4 (*sì*) sounds like *death*; words containing *si* or *sei* can trigger funeral connotations.
  - Number 8 (*bā*) sounds like *wealth/fortune* (fā).
  - Pinyin transliteration: How will the brand name be written in Chinese characters? (e.g. Coca-Cola brilliantly chose *Kě Kǒu Kě Lè* meaning "Tasty and Joyful").
- **Japanese:**
  - Katakana phonetic grid: Japanese phonology lacks standalone consonants (except *n*). Consonant clusters are broken up with vowels (*Stripe* becomes *Su-to-rai-pu*).
  - Avoid clumsy consonant stacks that become 5-syllable tongue-twisters in Katakana.

### D. Arabic & Semitic Languages
- **Arabic:**
  - Arabic lacks the /p/ sound; native speakers substitute /b/ (*Pepsi* is pronounced *Bebsi*). Ensure names do not depend entirely on a distinction between /p/ and /b/.
  - Watch out for words resembling *haram* (forbidden), *khara* (feces), or *shaitan* (devil).

---

## 3. The 4-Step Global Vetting Protocol

1. **Phonetic Transparency Check:** Does the name contain phonemes that don't exist in target international markets? (e.g. English /th/, retroflex /r/).
2. **Reverse Slang Dictionary Search:** Query Urban Dictionary, Wiktionary, and foreign slang repositories for candidate name stems.
3. **Katakana / Transliteration Simulation:** Write out how the name would be pronounced in Japanese, Spanish, and Mandarin.
4. **Tone & Sacred Concept Review:** Ensure the word does not trivialise cultural, religious, or indigenous sacred terms.
